WHY THE COMPOSED VALUE IS BETTER THAN THE DECLARED ONE

1. The declared inertials came from an upstream Onshape export at densities
   nobody recorded, and they are demonstrably NOT one convention. Measured this
   session: head_pitch_to_yaw's declared mass implies 1250 kg/m3 over its mesh,
   foot_assembly's implies 748, and hip_roll_assembly's printed remainder about
   650. Three different conventions inside one robot. Whatever those numbers
   are, they are not a model of how these parts will be manufactured.

2. The composed value is built from MEASURED per-part mass at the process this
   project actually chose (fdm-asa, 3 perimeters / 20 % infill, Task M1), via
   scripts/part_mass_table.json. It is re-derivable: change the process, re-run
   --emit-table, re-run this composer, and the plant follows.

3. The composer's arithmetic is verified independently of its inputs.
   `--verify-composer` forces printed density to 1250 kg/m3 and reproduces the
   three pure-printed bodies to 0.999 on BOTH mass and every principal moment.
   Those bodies contain no bought part, so any error in the parallel-axis
   summation, the frame handling or the de-duplication would show up there.

4. The change is small and in the direction the evidence points. +2.7 % is well
   inside the 2.50-2.76 kg mass-DR band already recommended to Task M0/R2, and
   the new total 2.729 kg sits inside that band rather than moving it.

WHAT THIS ACCEPTANCE DOES NOT CLAIM

a. NINE OF 48 PART ENTRIES CARRY AN `ASSUMED` SOURCE — bms, board, bno055,
   holder, power_switch, roll_bearing, sg90, usb_c_charger, antenna. No mass for
   any of them exists anywhere in this repo and no hardware exists to weigh, so
   they are priced from mesh volume at the process density. They are small, but
   they are guesses and they are counted at the top of this report on purpose.

b. SERVO INERTIA IS VOLUME-SPLIT, NOT MEASURED. One STS3250's 74.5 g is divided
   across its five case meshes in proportion to volume. That reproduces the
   servo's total mass (neck_yaw_assembly: 111.29 g composed vs 111.31 declared,
   0.02 %) but NOT its inertia distribution -- a real servo's motor and gearbox
   are dense and concentrated, and the same body's principal moments come out at
   0.906-0.981 of declared. This is why neck_yaw_assembly is gated on mass and
   only REPORTED on inertia. Fixing it needs a servo mass-distribution model
   this repo does not have; Task S.8 (servo torque/current/thermal envelope) is
   where real hardware could supply one.

c. THE ABSOLUTE BASELINE IS STILL NOT SETTLED BY WEIGHING. Nothing here has been
   on a scale. This replaces one derivation with a better-founded derivation.
